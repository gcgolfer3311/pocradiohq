The Basics

What is a POC radio, exactly?

PoC stands for Push-to-Talk over Cellular. Instead of transmitting on licensed radio frequencies like GMRS, ham, or CB, a POC radio sends your voice as data over the existing 4G/LTE cellular network — the same way a phone call works, but instant, one-to-many, and walkie-talkie style.

🌎

Nationwide range

Anywhere you have cell signal, you have range — cross-country, not just cross-town. No repeaters, no line-of-sight limits.

🪪

Zero licensing

No FCC exam, no callsign, no GMRS registration fee. Charge it, insert the SIM, push the button.

📶

Needs a signal

The tradeoff: no cell coverage means no radio. Dead zones, deep backcountry, and disasters that knock out towers are POC's weak spot.

🛰️

GPS & dispatch

Most POC radios include live GPS location sharing and web-based dispatch/fleet tracking — a feature RF radios can't match.

FAQ

Common questions

What is a POC radio? +

A Push-to-Talk over Cellular radio is a handheld or mobile device that delivers instant walkie-talkie-style voice communication over 4G/LTE cellular data instead of licensed radio frequencies — typically with nationwide or global range and no FCC license required.

Do POC radios require a license? +

No. Because they run on cellular data rather than licensed radio spectrum, there's no FCC license, exam, or callsign needed to operate one in the US.

Do POC radios need a monthly subscription? +

Most consumer models ship with a bundled data SIM that's free for the first year or the life of the device, depending on the carrier partnership. Enterprise dispatch platforms like Hytera Horizon or Motorola WAVE PTX typically charge a per-seat monthly fee for the dispatch/admin console.

Is a POC radio better than GMRS or ham radio? +

They solve different problems. POC gives you range anywhere there's cell coverage, even nationwide, but goes dead without a signal. GMRS, ham, and CB work with zero infrastructure but are limited to line-of-sight or repeater range. Most serious users end up carrying both — POC for daily nationwide reach, RF as the off-grid backup.

Can I use a POC radio during a grid-down emergency? +

Only if cell towers stay up — POC radios rely entirely on the cellular network. For true grid-down redundancy, pair a POC radio with a GMRS or ham radio as backup, or look at dual-mode devices like the GLOBAL-PTT 5288 Plus that combine PoC with analog VHF/UHF.

Radio Hobbyists

POC for the radio enthusiast

Already licensed? Into shortwave, GMRS, or ham? POC radios aren't a replacement for RF — they're a powerful addition to your kit. Here's how hobbyists are using them.

🏔️

SOTA & POTA backup

Use a PoC radio as your nationwide coordination layer while you operate HF or VHF on a summit or park. No repeater needed — your base camp can reach you anywhere there's cell signal.

📡

Zello for ham nets

Many ham clubs run linked Zello nets alongside their repeater systems. A PoC radio lets you participate from anywhere in the country without finding a linked repeater or setting up EchoLink.
