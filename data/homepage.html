The Basics

What PoC is, and what it isn't.

01 · Range

Anywhere with signal

Your voice travels as data over 4G/LTE, so two radios in different states can talk like they're in the same room.

02 · License

None needed

No FCC exam, callsign or GMRS fee. Charge it, put in the SIM, press the button.

03 · The catch

No signal, no radio

Dead zones, deep backcountry and tower outages stop PoC cold. That's why many users carry an RF radio too.

FAQ

Common questions.

What is a PoC radio?

A PoC (Push-to-Talk over Cellular) radio is a walkie-talkie that sends your voice as data over the 4G/LTE cell network instead of over licensed radio spectrum. Range is anywhere both radios have cell signal, and no FCC license is required.

Do PoC radios require a license?

No. PoC radios use cellular data, not licensed radio spectrum, so there is no FCC license, exam or callsign needed in the United States. Dual-mode radios that also transmit on FRS are license-free too; GMRS or ham transmit features follow their own rules.

Do PoC radios need a monthly subscription?

It depends on the radio. Many consumer radios include a SIM free for the first year, then charge a small yearly renewal; for example TIDRADIO publishes $9.99 per year and Radtel quotes about $10 per SIM per year. Some sellers advertise lifetime free data. Enterprise dispatch platforms usually charge per user per month.

How much delay is there when you press PTT?

Every PoC radio has some delay because your voice is uploaded and relayed through a server. On the Radtel P8 we measured about 1 to 2 seconds. Delay grows on weak or congested signal, so press PTT and pause a beat before speaking.

Will a PoC radio work in a grid-down emergency?

Only while cell towers and their backup power stay up. For real off-grid backup, carry a GMRS or ham radio as well, or choose a dual-mode radio such as the TIDRADIO TD-M2 Pro, which adds license-free FRS for short-range talk with no network.
