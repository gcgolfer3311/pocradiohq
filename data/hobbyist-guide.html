📻 POC radio for ham operators & radio hobbyists

If you're already licensed — or already deep into shortwave, GMRS, or CB — a POC radio isn't a replacement for what you have. It's a layer you've been missing: instant nationwide push-to-talk with zero infrastructure dependency, no repeater hunting, no linked system drama. Here's how the radio hobby community is actually using them.

Why radio hobbyists are adding POC to their kit

The RF world has always had one limitation that no amount of power, antenna gain, or repeater linking fully solves: geographic dependency. Your GMRS radio is great until your group spreads across three states. Your linked repeater system is reliable until the trustee moves or the internet connection at the repeater site goes down. Your HF radio reaches the world — when band conditions cooperate.

POC radios solve a specific, narrow problem very well: reliable, nationwide, push-to-one or push-to-all voice communication over existing infrastructure you don't maintain. They fail at what RF radios do well (grid-down operation, long range without cell coverage, low latency in noisy RF environments). The hobbyists getting the most out of POC aren't replacing their RF gear — they're filling the gaps it leaves.

🏔️ SOTA & POTA: POC as your coordination layer

Summits on the Air and Parks on the Air activations have a logistics problem that POC radios solve cleanly. Your operator is on a summit with HF and VHF gear running contacts. Your base camp or chase team needs to coordinate — timing, band changes, incoming weather, extraction plans. Cell voice calls work but they're one-to-one and interrupt your operating flow. A POC radio running a shared group channel lets base camp broadcast to everyone simultaneously without pulling the summit operator off the air.

The range advantage matters for SOTA specifically. If you're activating in a region with patchy repeater coverage, a GMRS or ham FM handheld may not reliably reach your vehicle. A POC radio reaches your team the same way regardless of whether they're 2 miles away or 200.

📡 Zello for ham nets — how clubs are using POC

Zello has become the de facto POC platform for amateur radio communities. Many clubs now run parallel operations: a traditional linked repeater system for RF-accessible members, and a Zello channel for members who are traveling, out of repeater range, or simply prefer the reliability of cellular over RF propagation.

The advantage for club nets is coverage without compromise. A member driving through a dead zone for your repeater doesn't drop off the net — they stay connected through Zello on their POC radio or phone. The net control operator broadcasts to both the repeater and the Zello channel simultaneously.

For clubs considering this setup: the Inrico T320 is the standard recommendation because it runs Zello natively on Android, has the largest community of ham users, and the deepest accessory ecosystem. Members who already use Zello on their phones can participate without buying new hardware.

🛡️ Prepper & emergency preparedness — dual-mode is the play

The emergency preparedness community has debated POC vs. RF for years. The honest answer: both, for different scenarios.

POC wins when: Cell infrastructure is up. Nationwide coordination. Group members are dispersed. You need GPS tracking. No one wants to manage frequencies and repeater access.

RF wins when: Cell towers are down or overloaded. You're off-grid. You need to communicate with people who don't have a POC radio. You need true infrastructure independence.

The dual-mode devices make this debate irrelevant. The GLOBAL-PTT 5288 Plus carries both a PoC radio and an 8W analog VHF/UHF transmitter — you get cellular range for daily use and a real RF fallback when towers go down. For preppers who want one device that covers both scenarios, this is the pick.

Frequently asked questions

Can a ham radio operator use a POC radio? +

Yes — no license is required to operate a POC radio since it uses cellular data, not licensed radio spectrum. Ham operators use POC radios as a nationwide coordination layer alongside their HF/VHF/UHF equipment, particularly for SOTA/POTA activations and linked nets.

Is a POC radio better than a linked repeater for ham nets? +

They solve different problems. A linked repeater depends on infrastructure that can fail. A POC radio works anywhere there's cellular coverage with no dependency on trustee-maintained hardware. Most clubs that are using both run POC as the primary coordination channel and the repeater as the RF backup.

What is the best POC radio for shortwave listeners? +

The ChoYong WT2 is the standout for SWLs — TEF6686-chip SW/AM/FM receiver plus full PoC PTT and 50,000-station internet radio in one handheld. We've tested it hands-on. Read our full review.

Do I need a license to use a POC radio in a ham net? +

No license is required for the POC radio itself. Whether the net itself requires participants to be licensed depends on how the net is structured — a ham club running a Zello net may require members to be licensed as a club rule, but the radio hardware has no legal license requirement.
