🔋 I just bought a POC radio. Now what?

Activation · App Setup · Channels · Range Testing · Troubleshooting

The radio's out of the box, it's charging, and you have no idea what to do next. This is the walkthrough — five steps that get any POC radio talking, plus model-specific setup notes and fixes for the problems people actually run into.

The 5-step setup, start to finish

Charge fully before first power-on
Give it a full charge — most POC radios ship at a partial charge and the first network registration draws more power than normal standby. A full charge also avoids a mid-setup shutdown.
Power on and confirm SIM activation
Power on somewhere with strong signal — near a window or outdoors, not a basement. Most radios ship with a pre-activated data SIM; look for a signal bar or "connected" indicator on the screen or in the app within the first 2–3 minutes. No signal after 5 minutes usually means a SIM or activation issue — see troubleshooting below.
Install and pair the correct app
Not every radio needs one. Check the model-specific notes below — Android-based radios (Inrico, AnySecu) need Zello, TIDRADIO needs Odmaster, and self-contained radios (ChoYong, Hytera, Motorola) manage channels without a separate app.
Create your first channel and add your people
Set up one channel, name it something you'll remember, and send join invites or share the channel code with whoever you're communicating with. Start with one channel — you can always add more once the basics work.
Test before you rely on it
Do a real test call with the other person before a trip, shift, or event — not the day of. Confirm audio is clear both directions, the PTT button responds without lag, and battery holds up over a couple hours of standby.

Common first-time problems, fixed

📶
Radio won't get a signal after power-on. Move near a window or outside, power cycle, and wait a full 3 minutes — first-time network registration is slower than normal reconnects. If it's WiFi-based, confirm you're on 2.4GHz, not 5GHz.
🔇
Other person can't hear you, or audio cuts out. Usually a weak connection on one end, not a hardware fault — check both radios show full signal bars before assuming it's broken. Background wind noise on handhelds is normal; cup your hand near the mic in windy conditions.
🔘
PTT button doesn't do anything. On Android-based radios, this almost always means the button isn't mapped in the app yet — check Zello's Hardware Button settings. On self-contained radios, confirm you're actually on a channel and not still in a menu screen.
👥
Can't find or add the other person to your channel. Both people need the exact same app and, for private channels, the exact same channel name or invite code — small typos create a separate channel that looks identical.
🔋
Battery drains much faster than expected. Standby on cellular data draws more than WiFi — if you're near WiFi most of the day, connect to it instead of relying on cellular. Screen brightness and always-on displays are the other big drains on Android-based models.

Before you rely on it — a quick checklist

  • Full charge confirmed, not just "charging" light on
  • Signal/SIM status shows connected, not searching
  • App installed and signed in (if your model needs one)
  • PTT button tested and mapped correctly
  • Test call completed with the other person — both directions
  • Channel name and invite code written down somewhere you won't lose it
