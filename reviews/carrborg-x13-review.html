Value for price

This is the X13's strongest argument. Most 4G LTE PoC radios in this price bracket carry an ongoing data subscription — $9.99/yr on the low end, $30+/month on some enterprise-oriented models. CARRBORG bundles a pre-activated SIM with "lifetime free" global data into the purchase price, which means the $130–180 you pay up front is effectively the total cost of ownership, not just the entry fee.

Weighed against a screen this bright, voice this clear, and a battery this large (4000mAh, USB-C), the X13 earns an 8/10 for value — it undercuts subscription-based competitors on total cost while still delivering a genuinely usable device, not a stripped-down bargain-bin radio. The trade-offs are latency and size, not the core experience.

Buying tip: Prices swing noticeably between the CARRBORG official site, Amazon, and eBay — we saw listings from roughly $125 to $230 for what's functionally the same X13 hardware. Compare current listings before buying rather than assuming one retailer is cheapest.

Screen and everyday usability

The X13's display is the standout hardware feature. It's noticeably brighter and easier to read at a glance than most radios in this category — outdoor visibility in direct sun is genuinely good, which matters for a device meant to be checked quickly mid-task rather than studied indoors. Menu text, channel names, and signal indicators are all legible without squinting or tilting the screen to kill glare.

That screen quality carries into the overall user experience. Setup and channel navigation are straightforward — this isn't a device that buries basic functions three menus deep. Anyone picking up the X13 for the first time should be talking on it within a few minutes, no manual required.

Voice clarity

Transmitted and received audio is clear and full — voices come through without the tinny, compressed quality that shows up on cheaper PoC handhelds. In practical use, this matters more than almost any other spec: a radio with mediocre voice clarity gets abandoned regardless of what else it does well, and the X13 doesn't have that problem.

PTT latency — the honest downside

Here's where the X13 gives some of that goodwill back. Keying up and getting through carries a touch more lag than faster PoC radios we've tested, including the ChoYong WT2. It's not dramatic — this isn't a half-second delay that ruins conversations — but side by side with quicker competitors, the gap is noticeable, especially in back-and-forth exchanges where timing matters.

Who this affects most: If you're using the X13 for fast-paced coordination — event staffing, active job sites, anything with rapid call-and-response — the extra beat of lag is worth factoring in. For casual family, travel, or check-in use, it's a non-issue.

Size and ergonomics

The X13 is a large radio. That size is part of what makes room for the bright screen and the big 4000mAh battery, so it's a real trade-off rather than a pure downside — but it means the device can feel bulky in the hand, and users with smaller hands may find it harder to grip comfortably during extended use or one-handed operation.

If you've handled compact PoC handhelds like the ChoYong WT2 or AnySecu's smaller models, the size difference will be immediately obvious. Neither is objectively "correct" — it's a straightforward trade of pocketability for screen size and battery capacity.

Who should buy the CARRBORG X13

Buy it if: You want a bright, legible screen, clear voice quality, and a no-subscription cost structure, and you're not bothered by a larger handheld or a slightly slower key-up. It's a strong fit for casual, family, travel, or check-in style use where split-second timing isn't critical.

Skip it if: You have smaller hands and want a device you can comfortably one-hand all day, you need the fastest possible PTT response for fast-paced coordination work, or pocketability is a priority over screen size.

Verdict

The CARRBORG X13 is a good, honest mid-tier PoC radio, and the kind of device that's easy to recommend once you understand exactly what you're trading for what. The screen is genuinely one of the best in this price range — bright enough to read outdoors without fighting glare, which sounds like a small thing until you're the one squinting at a competitor's washed-out display in direct sun. Voice audio backs that up: clear, full, and free of the tinny compression that plagues a lot of budget PoC hardware. And the lifetime free data plan is a real, calculable savings over the life of the device, not a marketing footnote — most competitors in this bracket ask for an ongoing subscription that quietly adds up over a year or two of ownership, while the X13's total cost is essentially the sticker price.

Where it gives ground is exactly where our testing flagged it: keying up carries a touch more lag than quicker devices like the ChoYong WT2, and the body itself is large — big enough that it earns its bright, spacious screen, but also big enough that it won't sit comfortably in every hand or every pocket. Neither issue is severe on its own. The lag is a beat, not a stutter, and most casual-to-moderate PTT use won't run into it as a real problem. The size is more a matter of who's holding it — anyone with smaller hands, or anyone who prizes a slim, pocketable radio, should handle one in person or check dimensions carefully before buying, because this isn't a device that disappears into a jacket pocket the way some compact competitors do.

Put it all together and the X13 lands as a genuinely solid value pick rather than a class leader. It's not the fastest PoC radio we've tested, and it's not the most compact — but it doesn't need to win either category to be a smart buy. For anyone prioritizing screen visibility, voice clarity, and long-term cost over raw speed or pocket-friendliness, the X13 delivers real, dependable performance at a price that actually stays low once you account for the data plan most competitors make you keep paying for. It's an easy device to hand to a first-time PoC buyer, a family member, or anyone who just wants something that works clearly and simply without a subscription hanging over it — as long as they know going in that it's a bigger radio with a slightly slower trigger, not the smallest or snappiest thing on the market.

Rating: 4.0/5 — Recommended for buyers who value screen quality, voice clarity, and no-subscription pricing over speed and pocketability.
