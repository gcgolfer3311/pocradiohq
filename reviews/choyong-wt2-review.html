Who made this and why it matters

ChoYong built their reputation in the shortwave listening community with the LC90 and LC95 internet radios — compact, well-tuned receivers that punched above their price point. The WT2 is them applying that same formula to the POC radio market: take the categories that are underserved, stack them into one device, and price it aggressively.

What separates ChoYong from most Chinese radio brands is customer service. In testing and follow-up, their support team was responsive, knowledgeable, and actually helpful — a genuine differentiator in a category where most brands are unreachable after the sale. If something goes wrong with a WT2, you'll get a real answer.

Build quality and form factor

The WT2 is compact — noticeably smaller than most handheld POC radios. In hand it feels solid, not cheap: the housing has a quality fit to it, buttons have a satisfying tactile click, and nothing rattles. The overall finish is better than the price point suggests.

The one genuine criticism here is size. For daily carry or casual use the compact form factor is a feature. For extended PTT use with gloves, or for users with larger hands, the WT2 is a little small to hold comfortably for long stretches. A slightly larger body in the next generation would make this a near-perfect device.

Note: The WT2 does not run the Odmaster app. Channel and group setup is handled through the device's own interface — which is straightforward and quick to configure.

PTT performance — the most important test

Push-to-talk over cellular audio quality on the WT2 is excellent — clear, punchy, and with minimal latency. This is the core function of the device and ChoYong got it right.

Critical tip: Use 2.4GHz WiFi, not 5GHz. PTT performance on 2.4GHz is noticeably better — more stable connection, cleaner audio, less dropout. If your PTT sounds choppy, switching bands is the fix before you troubleshoot anything else.

Adding POC channels is easy and fast. The interface doesn't require a companion app — everything is configured directly on the device, which keeps setup accessible to anyone, not just tech-comfortable users. Group calling works reliably once channels are set up.

Analog UHF — useful as a backup, not a primary

The WT2's analog UHF side transmits at 2 watts with a range of approximately 5km in open conditions. That's enough for local backup communication — a parking lot, a small venue, a neighborhood group — but it's not a replacement for a full-power GMRS radio.

UHF audio quality is weaker than the POC side, which is expected at this power level. Anyone buying the WT2 expecting analog UHF to compete with a dedicated 5W GMRS handheld will be disappointed. Frame it correctly — the analog side is a "no cell signal" fallback, and for that purpose it does its job.

If you regularly work in areas with zero cell coverage and need serious analog range, pair the WT2 with a dedicated GMRS radio. If you just want a safety net for occasional dead zones, the 2W UHF side covers it.

Shortwave, AM, and FM reception

The TEF6686 chip is the right call here — it's a well-regarded tuner in the shortwave listening community and it shows in real-world use.

Shortwave: Reception is good with an external loop antenna. Without one, you'll pick up the stronger signals but the TEF6686 really opens up when you give it something to work with. If you're a shortwave listener adding POC capability, the WT2's SW performance will meet your expectations — it's not a standalone dedicated receiver, but it's genuinely competent.

AM: Impressively sensitive for a device this size. AM performance is a pleasant surprise — the WT2 pulls in stations that smaller combo devices typically struggle with.

FM: Works like a charm. No issues, clean audio, solid lock on stations.

SW tip: Add a simple external loop antenna for a meaningful improvement in shortwave reception. The WT2 responds well to antenna upgrades — same principle as any TEF6686-based receiver.

Internet radio — the standout feature

This is where the WT2 genuinely surprises. Access to over 50,000 stations and podcasts worldwide, all streaming over WiFi or cellular data. The library covers international broadcasts, local stations from virtually every country, and a deep podcast catalog.

The AI voice search makes the library actually usable. Instead of scrolling through thousands of stations, you speak your search — country, genre, station name, language — and the AI surfaces relevant results immediately. In practice this is fast and accurate, and it turns what could be a frustrating feature into the most enjoyable part of using the device.

"Listen to radio from all around the world" isn't marketing copy with the WT2 — it's exactly what the device delivers. BBC World Service, Radio Japan, stations from countries most receivers can't pull in on shortwave — all available on demand."

For anyone who already listens to internet radio and wants a dedicated handheld device rather than a phone app, the WT2 is a compelling standalone choice on this feature alone — before you even factor in the POC and SW capabilities.

Battery life

Battery performance earns a solid 8 out of 10. For a device running four different radio modes plus internet streaming, the battery life is decent — a full day of mixed use (PTT calls, SW listening, internet radio) is achievable with reasonable usage habits. It's not exceptional, but it's not a liability either.

Heavy internet radio streaming will drain faster than light PTT-only use, as expected. If you're planning an all-day event or an extended field use, bring a USB-C power bank as backup.

Pros & cons

Pros

  • PTT audio quality is excellent — clear, low-latency, reliable on 2.4GHz WiFi
  • 50,000+ station internet radio with AI voice search is the best feature in its class
  • TEF6686-chip AM/FM/SW receiver is genuinely competent, especially AM sensitivity
  • Easy channel and group setup directly on the device — no companion app required
  • ChoYong customer service is exceptional — responsive and actually helpful
  • USB-C fast charging
  • Compact form factor — easy daily carry

Cons

  • Body slightly too small for extended use with larger hands or gloves
  • Analog UHF at 2W is a backup only — not a substitute for a full-power GMRS radio
  • SW reception benefits from an external loop antenna — not a great barefoot SW performer
  • PTT performance noticeably better on 2.4GHz WiFi — requires user awareness of which band they're on

Who should buy the ChoYong WT2

Buy it if: You want nationwide POC range, a real shortwave/AM/FM receiver, and the best internet radio experience available in a handheld device — all in one unit. Also the right choice if you're a shortwave listener who wants to add PTT capability without carrying a second device.

Skip it if: Your primary need is high-power analog radio (get a dedicated GMRS handheld), or you need a dispatch platform with fleet GPS tracking (look at Hytera or Motorola), or you require a ruggedized IP-rated device for harsh environments.

Verdict

The ChoYong WT2 does something rare: it lives up to the "all-in-one" label. Most multi-mode devices compromise every function to fit them together. The WT2 delivers genuinely good PTT performance, a credible shortwave receiver, and an internet radio experience that's the best in any handheld at this price — in a compact package with excellent customer support behind it.

The compact size will be a minor annoyance for some users, and the 2W analog UHF side is best understood as a backup rather than a primary radio. But for anyone who wants maximum capability in minimum space — and who will actually use the internet radio and SW sides, not just the PTT — the WT2 is a genuinely impressive device and an easy recommendation.

Rating: 4.5/5 — Highly recommended for radio enthusiasts, travelers, and anyone who wants POC plus a real receiver in one device.
